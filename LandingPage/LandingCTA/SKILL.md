# Landing CTA & Conversion Copy

Turn landing page text from "describes the product" into "sells the product". Every rule below is backed by published research (Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report — 57M conversions, NN/g eyetracking, Baymard form studies, CXL social proof research) or practitioner canon (Julian Shapiro's handbook, Harry Dry's Marketing Examples, Copyhackers). Effect sizes from single vendor A/B tests are directional hypotheses, not guarantees — say so when the user asks "will this definitely lift conversion?".

## Core model

Hold the whole page to four principles:

1. **Purchase Rate = Desire − (Labor + Confusion)** (Julian Shapiro). Every sentence must raise desire or cut labor/confusion. If it does neither — delete it.
2. **Rule of One** (Copyhackers): one reader, one big idea, one promise, one offer per page. The copy-level twin of the 1:1 attention ratio — one goal, one place to click. Removing nav links alone has produced +40–100% lifts (Unbounce, VWO).
3. **Message match**: the headline must restate the ad/link/post that brought the visitor. Cold traffic from "AI invoice tool" ads must land on a headline about AI invoices, not the company mission.
4. **The in-person test** (Harry Dry): "Would this help me sell if I met the customer face to face? If not, remove it."

Users read at most ~20–28% of words on a page (NN/g), and 5th–7th grade reading level converts 11.1% vs 5.3% for professional-level prose (Unbounce CBR). Write shorter, simpler, sooner.

## Route by awareness stage first

Before picking formulas, place the visitor on Schwartz's awareness scale — it decides the framework and how much page you need:

| Visitor is… | They know | Framework | Page emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing yet | AIDA / story-led | long page, educate the pain first |
| Problem-aware | the pain, not solutions | **PAS** | agitate, then reveal the mechanism |
| Solution-aware | solutions exist, not yours | BAB (before–after–bridge), mechanism-led | differentiate: "the {category} that {differentiator}" |
| Product-aware | your product, undecided | comparison, proof, offer | testimonials, "us vs them", pricing clarity |
| Most aware | wants it | offer + friction removal | short page, CTA + risk reversal |

Cold paid traffic is usually problem/solution-aware; retargeting and branded search are product/most-aware. This is also why message match matters — the headline must meet the visitor at their stage.

## Headlines

Test: *if the visitor reads only the headline, do they know exactly what you sell and why it's better?* Vague slogans ("Supercharge your workflow!") fail.

Build it in two steps: (1) state the value as a concrete outcome, (2) add a hook — a bold specific claim or the biggest objection killed.

| Formula | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome + objection killed | {End result}. {Objection killed}. | "Visually design and develop sites from scratch. No coding." (Webflow) |
| X without Y | {Desired outcome} without {pain} | "Groceries delivered in 1 hour. Say goodbye to traffic and lines." |
| Category + differentiator | The {category} that {differentiator} | "The inbox that writes replies for you" |
| Social-proof-led | How {N} {audience} {get outcome} | "How small brands sell more online" + "18,000+ reviews" (Privy) |
| How-to / imperative | {Verb} {outcome} | "Create a webpage for your business" |
| Question | {Pain question} | "Still doing payroll in spreadsheets?" — only when the answer is obviously "yes, and it hurts" |
| Case-study | How {company} got {result} in {time} | "How Shopify lifted conversions 35% in 30 days" |

Rules of thumb:
- Benefits beat features: "Save hours automating recurring tasks" > "Automated workflow management with advanced integrations".
- The **caveman test**: a stranger glancing for 3 seconds should be able to grunt back what you sell.
- Numbers beat adjectives: "loads in 0.3s" > "blazingly fast"; "18,432 teams" > "thousands of teams".

**Subheadline** = mechanism + audience, max 2 sentences. Pattern: `{Product} is a {category} that lets {audience} {achieve headline promise} by {mechanism}.` It makes the headline's promise believable.

**Above the fold, all five must be present** (Harry Dry): value (title) · how you create it (subtitle) · visualise it (product visual) · make it believable (proof) · make the next step easy (CTA).

## CTA buttons

- **Verb + specific value, 2–5 words**: "Get instant access", "Start my free trial", "Download the checklist". The button fulfills the headline's promise — a "call to value", not a call to action.
- Never "Submit", "Click here", or bare "Learn more" on a money page — they describe mechanics, not benefit.
- **First person tends to win**: "Get **my** free template" beat "Get **your** free template" by +90% CTR in the classic ContentVerve test (later replications +30–40%). Offer a first-person variant.
- **Append the biggest objection** when it fits: "Try Baremetrics free — no credit card".

**Microcopy under the button** is the highest-leverage line on the page. Stack 1–3 risk reversals: "Free 14-day trial · No credit card · Cancel anytime" / "Setup in 2 minutes" / "GDPR compliant". If the trial doesn't require a card, always say so: no-card trials get 3–5× more signups (Chargebee, Totango data).

**Hierarchy**: exactly one primary action per page. Secondary CTA (usually "Book a demo" vs "Start free") is a ghost/outline button. Simple product → trial-first; complex/high-ACV product → demo-first.

**Placement cadence**: hero + final section at minimum. On long pages repeat after each belief-building block (features, testimonials, pricing) — roughly every 1.5–2 screens — and every repetition is paired with a restated reason, never a naked button. The final CTA is a full section: restate the core promise + button + risk reversal (a founder's note works well here: their problem → you own it → the happy ending). On long mobile pages a sticky CTA that appears only *after* the hero CTA scrolls away has tested +5–10%.

## Forms

- **≤5 fields**, ideally name + email. Cutting 4→3 fields lifted conversions ~50% (HubSpot data). Every extra field must earn its qualification value.
- Long form unavoidable? **Multi-step** beats single-step (case studies +35% to +300%): start with easy low-commitment questions, ask for email **last**.
- Baymard rules: labels **above** fields (never placeholder-as-label — it vanishes on typing); inline validation fires on blur, not mid-typing; a failed submit preserves all input and scrolls to the error.
- The form headline states the value of submitting ("Get the free teardown"), never "Contact us".
- Waitlist pages: single email field + outcome headline + proof + referral loop on the confirmation page ("share to jump the queue" — the Robinhood pre-launch mechanic, ~1M signups).

## Social proof

Strength ladder: logo cloud → aggregate numbers → star ratings (G2/Trustpilot) → quotes → screenshotted tweets/Slack messages → mini case studies with numbers → named-customer video.

A strong testimonial has all four:
1. **Specific, quantified outcome** — "cut onboarding from 4 days to 6 hours", not "great tool!";
2. **Full attribution** — name, title, company, headshot (photos measurably improve recall — CXL, p=0.0035);
3. **Objection shape** — it answers a named fear ("I thought migration would take weeks — it took an afternoon");
4. **Scannable** — bold the key result phrase inside the quote.

Placement (two-zone rule): above the fold proof = *credibility* (logos, ratings, counts); below the fold proof = *action* (customers living the promised outcome). Map each quote to the objection raised by the adjacent section. Put the single strongest quote right next to the final CTA.

## Copy style pass

Run these edits over any draft:

- Front-load headings with the information-carrying words; ≤8 words (F-pattern scanning — users see the left edge and first words most).
- One idea per paragraph; bullets over prose; bold the phrases a skimmer must not miss.
- Replace every adjective with a number or a concrete noun where possible.
- Features are written as outcomes: `{Feature}, so you can {progress}`.
- Name the pain in the customer's own words before presenting the solution (PAS: problem → agitate → solve).
- Check reading level: target Flesch-Kincaid grade ≤7. Words of 3+ syllables correlate −24% with conversion.
- Ruthless cut pass: ask "what would a skeptical reader delete first?" and delete it yourself.

## Benchmarks (for honest answers to "what's a normal conversion rate?")

Unbounce CBR medians: overall **6.6%**; SaaS **3.8%**; finance 8.4%; ecommerce ~4.2%; events 12.3%. Email traffic converts best (~19%), paid search ~11%. Mobile is 83% of visits but converts ~8% worse — audit mobile first. Top-10% pages convert 3–5× the median. Webinar registration 25–45%; waitlists ~20% achievable; opt-in trials → paid ~9% vs card-required ~31% (of a 3–5× smaller pool).

Caveat to repeat to users: at mature experimentation orgs only ~1/3 of A/B tests win (Kohavi). Test big structural things first (offer, headline, form length), not button colors.

## Workflow

**Writing copy from scratch:** interview briefly (product, audience, traffic source, #1 objection, desired action) → draft 3 headline variants using different formulas → subheadline = mechanism + audience → CTA + microcopy → section copy top-down → style pass → present variants with one recommended and why.

**Reviewing an existing page:** audit in impact order — value-prop clarity → headline → CTA → visual hierarchy → trust/proof → objection coverage → friction (a weak headline outweighs any button tweak). Quote the exact weak lines, rewrite each in place, explain the principle behind every change in one short sentence. Deliver tiered: **Quick wins** (copy swaps, minutes) → **High-impact changes** (structure, new proof) → **Test ideas** (hypotheses with expected direction). For every rewritten headline/CTA give 2–3 alternatives with a recommendation and why.

### Final checklist
- [ ] Headline passes caveman test; message-matches the traffic source
- [ ] Subheadline = mechanism + audience
- [ ] All five above-the-fold elements present
- [ ] One primary CTA; verb+value copy; first-person variant considered
- [ ] Risk-reversal microcopy under every primary button
- [ ] CTA repeated after each proof block with a restated reason
- [ ] Form ≤5 fields or multi-step, labels above, validation on blur
- [ ] Testimonials: photo + name + number + objection-shaped, mapped to sections
- [ ] Strongest quote beside the final CTA
- [ ] Grade ≤7 reading level; numbers instead of adjectives; headings front-loaded
